# Fields

Suggested spines: a natural order of foundations in each field, used to choose "the next card" when the learner asks for one. They are a guide. The learner's own questions always come first, and a card can belong to any field, including a new one.

Each field lists its standard sources.

## Electronics
Charge and Coulomb's law → superposition → electric field → voltage as energy per charge → conductors → current → resistance and Ohm's law as a material property → capacitance → magnetic field of currents → induction → inductance → energy flow in circuits → when circuit rules hold → RC and LC behaviour → semiconductors and the diode → the transistor.
Sources: Feynman Vol. II; Purcell & Morin; Griffiths; MIT OCW 8.02 and 6.002; Horowitz & Hill.

## Power electronics
Why switch instead of burning voltage → the inductor as an energy store → buck converter → boost converter → volt-second balance → magnetics and saturation → switching losses → control loops.
Sources: Erickson & Maksimović; Kassakian, Schlecht & Verghese.

## Analog design
Linearity and small signals → the transistor as a voltage-controlled current → gain stages → feedback → op amps → frequency response → stability → noise.
Sources: Sedra & Smith; Razavi; Gray, Hurst, Lewis & Meyer; Horowitz & Hill.

## Chip making
Crystals and energy bands → why silicon → doping → the p-n junction → the MOSFET → oxidation → lithography → implantation and diffusion → etch and deposition → interconnect → scaling and its limits.
Sources: Sze; Pierret; Streetman & Banerjee; Plummer, Deal & Griffin.

## Robotics
Forces and torque → rigid-body motion → the electric motor → gears and transmissions → sensors → feedback control (PID) → kinematics of arms → dynamics → planning.
Sources: Kleppner & Kolenkow; Lynch & Park, Modern Robotics; Åström & Murray, Feedback Systems.

## Astrophysics
Gravity as an inverse-square force → orbits and Kepler's laws → light as an electromagnetic wave → spectra and temperature → how stars hold up against gravity → fusion → stellar lives and deaths → galaxies → expansion of the universe.
Sources: Carroll & Ostlie; Feynman Vol. I.

## Rockets
Momentum conservation → the rocket equation → specific impulse → nozzles → staging → orbital velocity → transfer orbits and delta-v → re-entry heating.
Sources: Sutton & Biblarz; Curtis, Orbital Mechanics for Engineering Students.

## Automobiles
Forces on a vehicle (traction, drag, rolling resistance) → power and energy needed → heat engines and their limits → electric motors and batteries → inverters → braking and regeneration → tyres and handling.
Sources: Gillespie, Fundamentals of Vehicle Dynamics; Heywood; Fermi, Thermodynamics.

## Mathematics
Rate of change (derivative) → accumulation (integral) → the exponential → vectors → linear maps and matrices → eigenvectors → differential equations → complex numbers and oscillation → probability → the normal distribution.
Sources: Strang (MIT 18.06); Apostol; Boas.

## How AI works
A model as a function with knobs → loss → gradient descent → neurons and layers → backpropagation as the chain rule → embeddings → attention → transformers → training at scale → where it is heading.
Sources: Goodfellow, Bengio & Courville; Bishop; Stanford CS229 and CS224n notes; original papers.
