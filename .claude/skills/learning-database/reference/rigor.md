# Rigor standard

Applies to every lesson, with no exceptions. A lesson that does not meet it is not accepted into the shared database.

1. **First principles, at the level of the governing laws.** Electronics is taught from electromagnetism: charge, fields, Maxwell's equations, energy in fields. Circuit rules (Kirchhoff, V = IR, component equations) are derived as approximations of the field description, with the conditions under which they hold. The same standard holds in every field: start from the governing laws (Newton, conservation laws, thermodynamics, Maxwell, quantum mechanics where needed) and derive.
2. **Dependency rule.** A lesson may rely only on results established in its prerequisite lessons (any field) or stated in its own "Starting points" section. If something is an experimental fact and not a derived result, say so.
3. **No analogy unless it is exact.** No water pipes, no "think of it like". A shared mathematical structure (for example the inverse-square laws of gravity and electrostatics) may be pointed out because it is exact. If a comparison would mislead at depth, leave it out.
4. **Precise and general.** Exact definitions, SI units on every number, stated assumptions, stated limits of validity. No simplification that later has to be unlearned.
5. **Authoritative sources only.** Anchor content to standard university texts and courses. No blogs, forums or popular web explainers.
6. **Cited and checked.** Every lesson ends with a Sources section naming chapter and section. Recompute every worked number. Where a source is openly available (feynmanlectures.caltech.edu, ocw.mit.edu, physics.nist.gov), check the key claims, formulas and constants against it before publishing. Write in original words; never copy passages.
7. **Say plainly when uncertain.** A claim that could not be checked is removed or marked as unchecked in the lesson.
8. **Interactives compute the real physics.** Real equations, SI units, real magnitudes. If a drawing is not to scale (for example arrows on a log scale), the caption says so.

## Source list

- **Electromagnetism:** Feynman Lectures Vol. II; Purcell & Morin, *Electricity and Magnetism*; Griffiths, *Introduction to Electrodynamics*; MIT OCW 8.02 course notes; Chabay & Sherwood, *Matter and Interactions* (surface charge in circuits).
- **Circuits and electronics:** Agarwal & Lang (MIT 6.002); Horowitz & Hill, *The Art of Electronics*; Sedra & Smith, *Microelectronic Circuits*; Razavi; Gray, Hurst, Lewis & Meyer.
- **Power electronics:** Erickson & Maksimović, *Fundamentals of Power Electronics*; Kassakian, Schlecht & Verghese.
- **Semiconductors and chips:** Sze, *Physics of Semiconductor Devices*; Pierret; Streetman & Banerjee; Plummer, Deal & Griffin, *Silicon VLSI Technology*.
- **Mechanics, robotics, control:** Kleppner & Kolenkow; Feynman Vol. I; Lynch & Park, *Modern Robotics*; Åström & Murray, *Feedback Systems*.
- **Thermodynamics and engines:** Fermi, *Thermodynamics*; Heywood, *Internal Combustion Engine Fundamentals*; Gillespie, *Fundamentals of Vehicle Dynamics*.
- **Astrophysics and rockets:** Carroll & Ostlie; Sutton & Biblarz, *Rocket Propulsion Elements*; Curtis, *Orbital Mechanics for Engineering Students*.
- **Mathematics:** Strang (MIT 18.06); Apostol or Spivak; Boas.
- **AI:** Goodfellow, Bengio & Courville; Bishop; Stanford CS229 / CS231n / CS224n notes; the original papers.

## Writing style

Plain, direct sentences. Define each term once and keep using the same word. No filler, no hype, no decorative metaphors.
